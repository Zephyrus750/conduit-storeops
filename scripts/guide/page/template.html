The Visual Guide, audited

The guide's four ways of reading it (Watch, Try it, Handbook, Paper) are good and worth keeping. What lets it down is what it shows. Every screen is redrawn by hand, so the drawings have fallen behind the app. It covers about a third of what Conduit does now. And someone outside a store can't open it. The fix I recommend is to record each chapter from the real app, so it stays accurate and sharp, and to build a public showcase edition from the same recordings.

    Sending it to someone

    Can't open it

    The guide lives in Help, so it opens only after signing in to a store. Its printed pages' QR codes open the store's own app too.

    Two copies

    The standalone Conduit Visual Guide page from 25 October is a separate copy of the code, and they have already drifted: its Backfill chapter is the older "scan a bay" one.

    Written for staff

    It teaches tasks to people who already work there. Someone deciding whether Conduit is worth it gets no "what is this", no view of the whole pipeline and nothing on what it saves.

    Worth keeping
    • Watch, Try it, Handbook and Paper
    • The ? on every screen that opens its chapter
    • The For picker and the seven languages
    • Developer notes with the event log, and the test that keeps them true
    What I recommend

    Record every chapter from the real app

    Not taken (7 November): the guide keeps drawing its screens. This section stays as the record of what was considered; the sample above was made this way.

    Stop drawing the screens. A demo store holds a believable day: trucks with manifests, a morning report, bays to backfill, shelves to refresh, cages, a recall. A capture script drives the real app through each chapter on a phone (390 × 844) and a store PC (1920 × 1080), and keeps each screen at 2× with the spot it tapped. The guide plays those frames. When a screen changes, one command re-shoots every chapter. If a button a step relies on has gone, its capture fails and names the step, so a stale chapter can't slip through.

    Demo storeOne seed script: a whole day of trucks, reports, bays and shelves, with no real staff.
    →
    CapturePlaywright plays each chapter in the real app, saving 2× frames, the tap spots and the captions.
    →
    In HelpThe same player: Watch, Try it on the real tap spots, Handbook, Paper.
    →
    Showcase editionA public page built from the same frames, for anyone, no sign-in.

    On the store PC's 1080p screen, a PC chapter can zoom in on the part that matters (the dock grid, a pallet card). Text stays readable on a laptop or a phone, which a drawn 880 px screen never managed.

    What it fixes

    • Accurate by construction: it shows the real screens
    • Sharp at 2×, with the real map and real data
    • Stays current with one command
    • Chapters become a list of steps, quicker to write than drawings
    • Small: about 40 KB a phone frame, 60 to 150 KB a PC frame

    What it costs

    • Screens are in English; captions and explanations stay in all seven languages
    • Help fetches a chapter's frames the first time it opens (cached after, like the translations)
    • A capture script needs updating when a screen's buttons change (it fails loudly when they do)

    What I ruled out

    • Draw better: it would drift again with the next change.
    • Freeze the real HTML: crisp, but every frame would carry the 800 KB map, slow to open.
    • A live demo store people click around: the best version, but a project of its own. The same demo store makes it a later step (a Practice mode).
    For people outside the store

    The showcase edition

    A page anyone can open from a link, with no sign-in, and no store's real data. It tells the story first, then lets the reader go as deep as they like.

    1 · What Conduit is

    One program for the floor, the stockroom and the back dock, replacing three. It runs on the phones, the store PCs and the dock screens the store already has, works without wifi, and speaks seven languages.

    2 · A day at the store

    The pipeline as a timeline, from the DC's manifest to a shelf: publish manifests → plan the week → the truck lands → receive → Take 5 → decant → close → the morning report → backfill scan → desk review → the floor's refresh and price checks. Each stage plays its chapter.

    3 · The chapters

    The same chapters as Help, by area and by person (floor, stockroom, dock, managers), with Watch, Try it and Handbook.

    4 · What it changes

    Facts only: one sign-in instead of three apps, every change on record, phones and PCs in step within seconds, paper that still works. Numbers such as clear times or decant rates only if you give me Busselton's.

    It comes in two forms: a link (the page, which can also play short clips) and a single file for email or a USB stick, stills only, about 15 to 20 MB for every chapter.

    The chapters

    From 15 chapters to 33

    About 180 steps in all, 4 to 10 a chapter, each as long as the task needs, and each chapter with its own What ifs, recorded like the three in the sample. A chapter keeps its old drawn version until its recording replaces it, so nothing goes missing on the way.

    Your decisions

    1. Keep drawing the screens.The guide stays drawn by hand, as it is now, and is not recorded from the app. The drawings are brought up to date with the real screens, and they get the sample's features: What ifs with what to know, and camera scanning. The recording tools stay in the repo (scripts/guide/) to check each drawing against the real screen.
    2. Busselton 1241.The guide and the showcase show Busselton with its real map. The day itself (trucks, manifests, pallets, D-numbers) is made up.
    3. Sending: still open.A public link (you share the page from its Share menu), a single file, or both.
    4. "What it changes": figures like a real store's.Clear times, cartons an hour and shelves refreshed, from the demo day. The page says they come from a demo day, so nobody takes them for Busselton's own results.
    5. Language: no longer a question.Drawn screens follow each person's language, as they do now.
    6. Help stays as it is until the new guide is approved.It is worked out on this private page first, and one final PR moves it into Help.
